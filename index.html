What happens

Glass floats on molten tin, entering at about 1,100°C and leaving as a solid ribbon at 600°C. Top rollers and line speed set the thickness.

What goes wrong

  • Tin condenses on the roof and drips back as top speck
  • Bath atmosphere upsets cause dross
  • Thickness and width changes take time to settle

Why defects are a fuel problem

The cause happened hours before the defect

Rejected glass has already burned its fuel. To stop it repeating you need the cause, and the cause sits hours upstream. We shift each upstream signal by the right time lag so cause and effect line up.

The real lag is different for each cause. A tin bath defect reaches the scanner quickly. A furnace cause can take many hours, since melting alone lasts up to 50 hours. We estimate the lags from your own history and your engineers check them against what they know.
